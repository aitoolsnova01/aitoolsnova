Top 100 AI Tools You Should Use in 2026

📅 July 23, 2026 ⏱ 12 min read 👤 AIToolsNova Team 🏷️ AI Tools

Here's a confession from someone who has actually tested tools for a living: I once kept a spreadsheet of every AI tool I'd tried. It crossed 100 rows in 2024. The number I actually used in any given week never exceeded nine. The other ninety-plus weren't bad tools — they were answers to questions I didn't have. So this "top 100" article is built differently: instead of a hundred names with a fluffy sentence each (you've seen that page; everyone's seen that page), it's the map of the whole territory — every category that matters, the two or three names that lead it, who genuinely needs it, and who should walk past. Use it like a directory, not a shopping list.

Chat and general assistants — everyone needs exactly one

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity (for cited research), plus free no-signup options like our AI Chat. The honest guidance: free tiers cover most people, differences are taste-sized for everyday use (Claude leans best for prose, Gemini for Google-workspace lives, Perplexity for research), and running two for different jobs is normal now. Walk past: paying for three overlapping subscriptions. Deeper comparison: our alternatives guide.

Writing and editing — the most crowded shelf

General drafting (any chat model or a free writer), grammar layers (Grammarly), style rewriters, SEO-writing suites (Jasper-class — mostly chat models wearing marketing suits at 10x the price). Who needs a paid one: teams producing daily volume with shared voice needs. Who doesn't: everyone else — the free tier plus a human edit outperforms the expensive tier minus one.

Images — four different jobs pretending to be one category

Generation (Midjourney for impact, DALL·E for obedience, Stable Diffusion for control, Ideogram for text-in-image), editing (Canva as default), cleanup utilities (background removal, compression, resizing — free, browser-based, used weekly by normal people), and stock replacement. Most businesses need the utilities constantly and generation occasionally; the industry markets the reverse proportion.

Video and audio — the fast-moving shelf

Generators (Sora/Runway/Kling-class: brilliant for B-roll and mood, treacherous for real products), editing automation (text-based editors, auto-captions — the genuinely transformative bit), avatars (Synthesia-class, for training content with disclosure), voice (ElevenLabs for cloning-with-consent and narration). Who needs it: anyone publishing video regularly. Who should wait: anyone hoping generation replaces filming real things that exist.

Code — the deepest productivity gains anywhere

Copilot/Cursor/Codeium for completions and edits, chat models for debugging and explanation. Free tiers exist across the board (students especially). The one rule that keeps the gains: never commit a line you couldn't have written slower yourself. Full habits: our coding guide.

Productivity, meetings and automation — where hours actually return

Meeting transcription (Otter/Fathom-class — the single most underrated purchase in this entire article), workflow automation (Zapier/Make for form-to-sheet-to-notification chains), note AI (Notion-class, if your notes already live there). Walk past: AI daily-planners and "second brain" tools that need more maintenance than they save — the category with the worst promise-to-delivery ratio on this page.

Business utilities — the unglamorous winners

The tools that quietly serve more people than any generator: PDF converting/merging/splitting (browser-local — private by design), QR codes (static, tested on three phones before printing), resume building, email drafting, meta tags, passwords. Free, no accounts, and collectively the most-used "AI-era" tools in ordinary life.

SEO and marketing — handle with gloves

Research suites (Ahrefs/Semrush at real scale), content scorers (useful once, homogenising as a habit), free fundamentals (Search Console + the utility tools above) which outperform paid tools for small sites. The category where buying tools most often substitutes for doing work. Details: our SEO tools guide.

How to actually use a map like this

Pick the one category matching this week's actual pain. Try the free leader on a real task. Adopt or discard within a fortnight. Repeat monthly, never exceeding roughly nine live tools — the spreadsheet's lesson, offered so you don't need your own hundred rows. The free toolbox here covers the utility layer in one bookmark; our free tools guide covers the rest of the £0 tier.
