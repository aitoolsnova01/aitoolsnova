What These Models Can and Cannot Shoot Today

Short atmospheric shots are where current generation genuinely delivers: a slow push through a landscape, an abstract texture, a product turning on a plinth. Under about eight seconds with a single subject and minimal camera movement, the output is frequently usable in a real edit.

Consistency across shots remains the hard problem. The same character generated twice will differ in face, clothing and lighting in ways that read as a continuity error, so narrative work still needs reference images, seed control and a tolerance for reshooting.

Prompt like a director rather than a writer. Shot type, lens, movement, lighting and mood produce far better results than describing a plot, because the model is matching visual patterns, not staging a scene.

Budget, Rights and the Rendering Reality

Credits vanish faster than people expect. A finished thirty-second sequence often costs twenty or more generations once you account for failures, and pricing pages quote the successful render. Test a tool on the exact shot you need before committing to an annual plan.

Commercial rights vary by provider and by plan tier, and some free tiers grant no commercial use at all. If the footage is going into client work or a monetised channel, read the licence and keep a copy of the terms as they stood on the day you generated.

Fitting Generated Footage Into a Real Edit

Generated clips rarely match each other or your existing footage straight out of the tool. Colour grading everything through a single correction pass is what makes a mixed sequence look intentional, and it hides a surprising amount of inconsistency between shots.

Cut around the flaws rather than trying to prompt them away. Most artefacts appear in the last second of a clip and around hands, faces and text. Trimming the tail, cutting on movement and keeping problem areas out of frame solves more problems than another twenty regenerations.

Sound does most of the perceptual work. A generated shot with well-matched room tone, foley and music reads as filmed footage to most viewers, while the same shot silent reads as synthetic immediately.

What to Look For When Choosing A video generator

  • Clip length and consistency. Most models hold coherence for only a few seconds. Check the real limit, not the marketing one.
  • Watermark policy. Free tiers frequently brand the output, which rules out client work.
  • Render time and queue. A two-hour queue changes how you plan a day.
  • Audio handling. Many generators produce silent video. Confirm whether you need a separate audio step.

Mistakes That Cost Video creators Time

Expecting a finished film. Current tools produce clips, not narratives. Plan to assemble and edit.

Complex motion prompts. Multiple moving subjects break coherence quickly. Simple camera moves work far better.

Ignoring disclosure rules. Realistic synthetic footage of people must be labelled on most major platforms.

More Questions Answered

How long can AI-generated videos be?

Most tools produce a few seconds per generation. Longer pieces are stitched from multiple clips, which is where consistency becomes the challenge.

Can I use AI video commercially?

Depends on the plan. Free tiers often prohibit it or add a watermark. Check the licence before a client project.

Does it include sound?

Usually not. Expect to add voiceover, music and effects separately.

Do I need to disclose AI-generated video?

Yes on most major platforms when the footage is realistic, and increasingly required by law in some regions.
