Best Free PDF Tools

📅 July 10, 2026 ⏱ 5 min read 👤 AIToolsNova Team 🏷️ PDF Tools

A landlord's agent once asked a tenant I know to send "the signed lease, proof of income, and both IDs as a single PDF, under 10MB, by 5pm". It's a small genre of modern panic: four files in three formats on two devices, one deadline, and a search results page full of "free" PDF sites that want accounts, cap you at two operations, or — the part people don't think about at 4:15pm — quietly receive a copy of your lease, payslips and passport on their servers. She made the deadline, using browser-local tools, and the afternoon taught her the complete free-PDF survival kit. Here it is, organised by the panics that summon it.

"Make these into one PDF" — merging

The single most demanded PDF operation, and the simplest: order your files (rename them 01, 02, 03 first — order mistakes cause every redo), merge, then scroll the result page by page before sending. A blank page from a bad scan in the middle of an application is a discovery you want to make before the deadline, not after. Note that merging doesn't compress: ten 2MB scans make a 20MB file, which brings us to the caps.

"It says the file is too large" — splitting and shrinking

Upload caps (5MB and 10MB are the common walls) meet scanned documents constantly. Two free fixes, in order of preference: if only some pages are needed, split and send the relevant range — pages 12–14 of the contract, not the whole 90; if all pages are needed, the weight almost always lives in scanned images, so recompress the source images before rebuilding, or split the file into part 1 and part 2, which every portal and inbox tolerates.

"Turn this into a PDF" (and back) — converting

Documents, images and text convert to PDF cleanly in-browser. The honest caveats: complex layouts (multi-column, form fields, heavy tables) are where every converter guesses — check each page of anything important — and a scanned PDF is a photograph of text, so converting it "to Word" yields images, not editable words. That's an OCR job, a different tool species. Fonts you don't have get substituted too, which is why the output occasionally looks subtly wrong; it's not you.

The privacy point the 4:15pm panic skips

What people process as PDFs — leases, payslips, passports, medical letters, contracts — is the most sensitive file traffic of ordinary life. Upload-based free sites are a data trade: your documents for their service, on retention terms nobody reads at 4:15. Browser-local tools change the deal — the file is processed on your device and never travels, which you can verify by going offline mid-operation. For this file category specifically, local isn't a preference; it's the difference between a tool and a leak.

The honest limits of free-and-local

  • Your device is the engine: a 150-page scan will choke a budget phone. Big files belong on laptops.
  • Password-protected files need unlocking with the password first — and any tool promising to bypass protection is a tool to close immediately.
  • Editing is not covered: redacting, reordering within pages, form-filling are editor jobs. And never "redact" by drawing black boxes in a viewer — the text underneath survives and is recoverable, a mistake with a long career in embarrassing institutions.
  • OCR, e-signatures and stamped certifications remain the legitimately paid tier when you need them formally.

The 5pm checklist, reusable

Rename in order → convert strays to PDF → merge → scroll every page → check the MB against the cap → split or shrink if over → send, and keep your copy. Total time once the tools are bookmarked: under ten minutes, no accounts, no uploads. The whole kit lives free on our tools page — and if the task involves images inside the documents, the image tools guide covers that half.

PDF hygiene

  • Compress images before converting — PDFs bloat on images
  • Name files with date: Invoice_2026-08-21.pdf
  • Keep a 1-page text version for search
