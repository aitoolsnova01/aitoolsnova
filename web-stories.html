What Web Stories Are

Web Stories are full-screen, tappable pages built on the AMP framework. They behave like the story format people already know from social apps, but they live on the open web at a normal URL, which means they can be linked, shared and indexed like any other page. Google surfaces them in Search, in Discover and in Images, and they load almost instantly because the format restricts what can slow a page down.

Each story here condenses one of our longer guides into a handful of screens. If you want the quick version of a topic while waiting for a train, the story is the better format; if you want the detail, every story links through to the full article.

Why We Publish in This Format

Long-form guides and stories answer different needs. A guide is something you read when you have decided to solve a problem and have time to work through it. A story is something you flick through when you are curious but not yet committed, and it is far better at conveying whether a topic is worth your attention at all.

The format also imposes useful discipline on us. A screen holds one idea and a sentence or two, so there is nowhere to hide padding. Compressing a two-thousand-word guide into eight screens forces us to identify what actually matters in it.

How to Read Them

Tap the right side of the screen to move forward and the left side to go back, or swipe if you prefer. Holding your finger down pauses any automatic progression, which is useful when a screen has more text than a glance can absorb. Most stories here run to eight screens and take under a minute.

They work on desktop too, appearing as a centred phone-shaped panel with arrows on either side. The experience is designed for mobile, which is where most people encounter them, but nothing is lost on a larger screen.
