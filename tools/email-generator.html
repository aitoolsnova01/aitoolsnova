Writing Emails That Get Replies

The subject line decides whether the message is opened, and specificity beats cleverness. 'Question about the March invoice' outperforms 'Quick question' because the recipient can judge relevance without opening it. Keep it under about fifty characters so it does not truncate on mobile.

Put the ask in the first two sentences. Long preambles explaining context before stating what you want are the single most common reason business emails go unanswered, because the reader files it under 'deal with later' and never returns.

One request per email. Messages containing three separate asks reliably get a reply addressing one of them, which means chasing the other two later.

Tone, Follow-Ups and Common Mistakes

Match formality to the relationship, not to the medium. Over-formal phrasing with a close colleague reads as distance; casual phrasing in a first approach to a stranger reads as presumption. When unsure, write slightly more formally and let the other person set the register.

Follow up once after a reasonable interval, and make the follow-up shorter than the original rather than longer. Forwarding your own email with 'bumping this' works better than restating everything.

Read generated drafts aloud before sending. AI-written email tends toward padding and stock courtesy phrases, and removing them typically cuts the length by a third while making the request clearer.

The emails this actually helps with

The awkward ones: chasing an unpaid invoice without sounding rude, following up after silence, apologising for a delay, declining politely. Everyone freezes on these; a decent draft in ten seconds breaks the freeze. For a quick "yes, 3pm works" — just type it yourself, it's faster.

Make the draft sound like you

  • Tell it the relationship: "client of 3 years" reads differently from "cold prospect".
  • Include the one fact that matters: invoice number, date, amount. A specific email gets answered; a vague one gets filed.
  • Cut the generated opener if it's flattery ("I hope this finds you well") and start at the point.

One warning

Never send a money-related or legally sensitive email without reading every line. The generator writes plausible text, and plausible is dangerous in a payment dispute. Check names, amounts and dates yourself — it will happily keep a placeholder if you gave it one.
