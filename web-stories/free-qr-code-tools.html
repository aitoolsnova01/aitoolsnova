Tool 2 of 5

QRCode Monkey

Logos and colors, still free

Add your logo and brand colors without paying. Two warnings: keep the contrast dark-on-light, and test heavily styled codes — every design flourish costs you scan reliability.

Logo embed High-res export Style ≠ scannable

Tool 3 of 5

Canva QR element

When the code lives inside a design

If the flyer is already in Canva, use its built-in QR app instead of importing a PNG that gets stretched. Watch that templates don’t shrink the code below 2×2 cm.

Inside your design No import blur Mind the size
For Canva users
Tool 4 of 5

Dynamic codes (Bitly & co.)

Editable links, scan counts — with a catch

Dynamic services let you change the destination after printing and count scans. The catch: free tiers cap codes or expire, and if the service dies, your printed code dies with it.

Edit after print Scan analytics Service risk
Read the free-tier terms
Tool 5 of 5

Chrome’s built-in QR share

Already on your computer

Chrome and Safari can turn the current page into a QR from the share menu. Fastest option for showing a link across the room. Low-res export — fine for screens, not for print.

Zero install Instant share Screen-only quality
For meetings, not posters
Lesson 1 of 2

Test before you print

One sample, three phones

Our code worked on screen and failed on matte paper in dim light. Print one sample, scan it with three different phones, then order 500. The sample costs pennies; the reprint didn’t.

Print one first 3 phones minimum Check dim light
Cheap insurance
