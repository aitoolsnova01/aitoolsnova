Step 1 of 7

Find the culprit

Open DevTools → Network → sort by size. It is almost always a hero image, not your code. Fix the biggest file first.
Step 2 of 7

Resize before compress

Do not ship a 4000px photo into an 800px slot. Resize to display size first, then compress — the order matters.
Step 3 of 7

Compress at 70–80

JPG quality 75 is invisible to most eyes and cuts 60–80% of the weight. Browser-side compressors mean the file never leaves your machine.
Step 4 of 7

WebP, with one exception

Converting big JPG/PNG to WebP saves another ~30%. Keep PNG only for screenshots and logos with sharp text — WebP can smear fine edges at low quality.
Step 5 of 7

Lazy-load the right things

loading="lazy" on below-the-fold images is one attribute. Do not lazy-load the hero image — that delays the first thing people see.
Step 6 of 7

Test on a real phone

Desktop Wi-Fi lies to you. Check the page on a mid-range Android over 4G — that is the connection most of your visitors have.
Step 7 of 7

The honest limit

Compression cannot save a page with forty images. Cut the gallery to the eight that actually sell the thing.
